1

Basic Sounds (せいおん)

The 46 basic hiragana. Each row shares a vowel (a/i/u/e/o), each column shares a consonant.

a-rowk-rows-rowt-rown-rowh-rowm-rowy-rowr-roww-row
a-row (a)あaかkaさsaたtaなnaはhaまmaやyaらraわwa
i-row (i)いiきkiしshiちchiにniひhiみmiーりriー
u-row (u)うuくkuすsuつtsuぬnuふfuむmuゆyuるruー
e-row (e)えeけkeせseてteねneへheめmeーれreー
o-row (o)おoこkoそsoとtoのnoほhoもmoよyoろroをwo
Pronunciation tips (vowels)
  • a: shorter and crisper than English "ah"
  • i: shorter than English "ee"; pull lips sideways
  • u: relax the lips, don't round them much
  • e: close to English "eh" but a bit shorter
  • o: round the lips; shorter than English "oh"

Note: ん (n) is its own sound and doesn't belong to any row or column.

2

Voiced Sounds (だくおん)

Basic sounds with a voicing mark (゛) or semi-voicing mark (゜) added.

g-rowz-rowd-rowb-rowp-row
a-row (a)がgaざzaだdaばbaぱpa
i-row (i)ぎgiじjiぢjiびbiぴpi
u-row (u)ぐguずzuづzuぶbuぷpu
e-row (e)げgeぜzeでdeべbeぺpe
o-row (o)ごgoぞzoどdoぼboぽpo
Pronunciation tip

じ・ぢ and ず・づ sound almost identical in modern Japanese.

Special Sounds

Three special sounds that give Japanese its distinctive rhythm.

SoundExampleRomajiExplanation
Small っ
(sokuon)		きってkitteBriefly pause before doubling the next consonant
ん
(hatsuon)		ほんhonA full beat on its own, a nasal "n" sound
Long vowel
(chouon)		おかあさん
おにいさん

oniisan
kuuki		Holds the vowel one extra beat. The written form varies: a-row→a, i-row→i, u-row→u, e-row→i/e, o-row→u (most common)
Pronunciation tips
  • っ: hold your breath for a beat before the next sound
  • ん: pronounce it as its own full beat, even on its own
  • Long vowels: just hold the vowel one extra beat — don't add a new sound
